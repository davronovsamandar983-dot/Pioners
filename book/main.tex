\topicopen{Before you start}{A short plan for working through the 880
questions without wasting a single one.}

\begin{essentials}[The routine]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Read the \textbf{Essentials} box at the top of the topic first. It is
        the whole toolkit that topic's 44 questions draw on --- nothing in the
        topic needs anything beyond it.
  \item Work \textbf{Module 1} straight through, in order. The order is the
        lesson: each question assumes the ones before it.
  \item Check Module 1 against the answer key before starting Module 2. Fix
        what you got wrong \emph{first} --- Module 2 is built on top of it.
  \item Work \textbf{Module 2}. It restarts at question 1 and runs harder,
        the same way the real adaptive second module does.
  \item Use the \textbf{Work Space} box. Student-produced response questions
        are lost far more often to arithmetic than to method.
\end{enumerate}
\end{essentials}

\vspace{4pt}
{\small\color{inkgray}
\textbf{Timing.} On test day each module gives you 35 minutes for 22
questions --- about 95 seconds per question. Once a topic feels solid, redo
its Module 2 under that clock.\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Answer formats.} Multiple-choice questions have exactly one correct
option out of four. Student-produced response questions accept a number:
enter fractions as fractions or as decimals, and never round an exact answer
you can write exactly.}
