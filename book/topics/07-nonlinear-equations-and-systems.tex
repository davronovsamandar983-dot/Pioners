\chapter{Nonlinear Equations and Systems of Equations}
\topicopen{Advanced Math}{Solve quadratic, radical, rational and absolute
value equations, decide how many real solutions an equation has, and solve a
system that pairs a line with a parabola or a circle.}

\begin{essentials}
A quadratic equation is $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with $a\neq 0$. Solve it by factoring,
by completing the square, or by the quadratic formula
\[ x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\,. \]
The discriminant $b^2-4ac$ counts the real solutions: positive gives two, zero
gives exactly one, negative gives none. To complete the square, move the
constant across and add $(b/2)^2$ to both sides. For a radical equation,
isolate the radical, square both sides, then substitute every candidate back
--- squaring can create extraneous roots. For a rational equation, clear the
denominators and discard any value that makes a denominator zero. Solve
$|A|=B$, with $B\ge 0$, by $A=B$ or $A=-B$. Pair a line with a parabola or a
circle by substitution; the resulting quadratic has exactly one solution ---
the line is tangent --- precisely when its discriminant equals $0$.
\end{essentials}
